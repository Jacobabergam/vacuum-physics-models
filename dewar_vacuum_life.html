IR-camera dewar vacuum life: outgassing, getter budget, heat load

A cooled IR detector lives inside a pinched-off vacuum dewar. After seal-off there is no pump — only what the bake left behind (water in adhesives, in the cold-shield black coating, on the metal), what the steel emits forever (H₂, a trace of CH₄), and a getter with a finite capacity ledger. This page tracks the warm pressure over 1 day → 30 years of sealed life, the gas-conduction heat load it puts on the cold space when the camera runs (area-dependent, and species-aware: the cold shield cryopumps water, so H₂ and CH₄ carry the load), and the cooldown-time penalty as the vacuum softens. Materials and getters are picked from datasheet-backed presets — there is no outgassing rate to type in.

What kills a sealed dewar. The budget is brutally small: 10⁻³ Torr in a 0.1 L volume is P·V ≈ 10⁻⁴ Torr·L ≈ 0.1 µg of water. Sources ranked by era: surface water and thin films go first (hours–days, and any competent bake removes them), adhesive reservoirs drain over days–months (τ₁ = 4L²/π²D with drying depth L = volume / vent area — a buried bond line venting at its perimeter is the slow one), then H₂ from the steel bulk owns the budget within days unless a getter absorbs it, and finally CH₄ — which no getter pumps — sets the ceiling of a well-built dewar.
The getter is a ledger, not a pump curve. A St707-class Zr-V-Fe getter holds getterable gas at its equilibrium floor (~10⁻⁷ Torr) until its capacity is spent: water is surface-limited (~2.4 Torr·L per gram) while H₂ dissolves into the bulk (~170 Torr·L/g), so this page keeps two ledgers. Every Torr·L of water the bake leaves behind is getter capacity spent — bake quality is measured in ledger margin, not in the pressure the getter hides.
Heat load is area physics. In the free-molecular regime the gas-conduction flux is q = α·Λ₀·P·ΔT per unit area — independent of the gap — so the load scales with the cold-shield area you paint and cool. Per Torr, the species are NOT equal: Λ₀ = 4.38 (H₂), 1.97 (CH₄), 1.18 (air) W·m⁻²·K⁻¹·Pa⁻¹. When cold, the shield cryopumps water (ice vapor pressure is below 10⁻¹⁰ Torr at 80 K, and still only ~10⁻⁶ at a 150 K HOT-detector setpoint), so the species-resolved load rides on H₂ + CH₄ — and can EXCEED the classic "treat it as air" textbook curve late in life, when CH₄ dominates. The air-equivalent toggle shows that classic curve (12 mW at 10⁻⁴ Torr, 116 mW at 10⁻³ for 40 cm²).
Why the coating is on the panel. A painted cold shield is a polymer film with real water content: 40 cm² of Aeroglaze Z306 at 60 µm holds ≈ 1.6 Torr·L — two-thirds of a 1 g getter's entire water ledger. It dries fast (thin film, τ₁ ≈ 2 h at 22 °C), so a competent bake clears it — but skip or shortcut the bake and the paint spends the ledger on day one. Acktar's inorganic film carries far less absolute water (3–5 µm thick) but physisorbs 1–4 wt% in ambient air; both coatings, and Z306 especially, re-load within 24 h of air exposure (that is literally what ASTM E595's WVR measures) — a bake only counts if the dewar is pinched off before it sees ambient humidity again.
Model, all constants from research/data/dewar_life_materials.json and outgassing_anchor_rates.json (sources per entry; representative values are flagged there and here): fixed context — shell 293 K, cold tip 80–150 K (slider; the HOT-detector trade: a higher setpoint buys cooler lift and ΔT margin, and above ~140 K ice barely accumulates), 5 mm gap, 0.1–0.5 L free volume, EOL criterion 10⁻³ Torr warm, cooler lift 0.6 W at 80 K / 3.0 W at 300 K (linear, tactical Stirling class, representative), 15 g Cu-equivalent cold mass, ε_eff 0.03, leads 0.7 mW/K. Adhesive and coating reservoirs are Fickian slabs (Crank ch. 4) with the exact two-stage bake→storage argument (X = (D_b·t_b + D_s·t_s)/L², Crank ch. 7); shared water diffusivity D(295 K) = 2×10⁻⁹ cm²/s, E_a = 0.45 eV (epoxy moisture literature; sharing it with the polyurethane film is a flagged assumption — thin films fully deplete in any competent bake, so coating results are D-insensitive). Water contents: Lord Z306 datasheet DS3017 (TML 1.0 / CVCM 0.02, E595-77; dry-film density 1.12 g/cm³ computed from its 27 wt% / 23 vol% solids at 0.95 kg/L) with WVR 0.3–0.9 % from the NASA GSFC outgassing database paint-only lots (median 0.6 % used here — e.g. GSFC14442: 1.00/0.02/0.57); Acktar vacuum black ESA/ISME09 qualification (WVR 2.0–3.8 %, CVCM ≤ 0.01, film 3–5 µm at ~1.8 g/cm³); EPO-TEK H70E (WVR 0.25, GSFC12127) and 353ND (WVR 0.35, GSFC29110); Stycast 2850FT (WVR 0.05–0.15, ρ 2.29 — the driest preset); 3M 2216 gray (WVR 0.23 TDS / up to 0.51 across GSFC lots; 0.30 used). The WVR-as-moisture proxy is a 24 h rehydration figure and therefore biased LOW vs long-soak equilibrium — the generic epoxy preset (1 wt %) is the conservative anchor. Surface water: 3 monolayers over a 0.75–1.15 eV Redhead window, ν = 10¹³ s⁻¹. Fixed gases: H₂ 10⁻¹² Torr·L·s⁻¹·cm⁻² (band 10⁻¹³–3×10⁻¹², Chiggiato), CH₄ 10⁻¹⁵ (decade uncertainty — it propagates 1:1 into the gettered-life ceiling). Getter: Sandia St707 data (H₂ 170 Torr·L/g) and the PSEC H₂O-capacity estimate (2.4 Torr·L/g, surface-limited); gram options and the 10⁻⁷ Torr floor are representative, and the ledger ignores speed derate near saturation. Gas conduction: Corruccini free-molecular form bridged to the continuum plateau k·ΔT/gap by a parallel sum. Cooldown: Debye c_p(Cu), θ_D = 343 K, RK4; stall = net refrigeration reaching zero while warm. A real leak is excluded — if one exists it dominates everything and the sealed model is void (see the leak-diagnosis write-up in analysis/). Differences from the dewar_vacuum_model package: dual getter ledger (vs a single capacity), one lumped adhesive reservoir (vs two named entries), 400-point energy grid, and the species-resolved cold-load accounting is new. Python parity twin: python3 dewar_vacuum_life.py --check (REQ-U2, ≤0.5 %).

Where the water budget stands — live from the panel settings

Water at pinch-off is what the selected bake failed to remove. Every Torr·L of it is either getter water-ledger spent (gettered dewar) or pressure on day one (no getter) — and during cold operation it ends up as ice on the coldest optic (~0.1 µm on the cold filter is measurable transmission loss, ~1 µm is severe in the 3 µm band).
