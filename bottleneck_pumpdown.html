Bottleneck-limited pumpdown: viscous vs. molecular flow

A turbopump hangs off a small manifold, connected to the chamber you actually care about through a narrow tube. Near atmosphere the tube conducts like a fire hose and the pump is the limit; once the mean free path outgrows the tube bore, conductance collapses to a fixed geometric value and the tube — not the pump — sets how fast the chamber empties.

Why the bottleneck stops making sense at high vacuum. Gas flow through a tube is characterized by its conductance C (L/s), and the pump speed the chamber actually feels is the series combination S_eff = (1/S + 1/C)⁻¹ — the smaller of the two always wins.
Viscous regime (λ ≪ d): molecules collide with each other constantly, so gas moves as a bulk fluid driven by pressure gradient — Poiseuille flow, C ≈ 180 · d⁴ · P̄ / L (cm, Torr → L/s). Conductance is proportional to pressure and is enormous near atmosphere, so the tube is invisible and the pump is the limit. Your fluid-flow intuition works here.
Molecular regime (λ ≳ d): a molecule crosses the whole tube without meeting another molecule. There is no "flow" to push — each molecule wanders by wall bounces and only by chance finds the exit. Conductance collapses to a pressure-independent, purely geometric value C_mol ≈ 12.1 · d³ / (L + 4d/3), which for a narrow tube can be orders of magnitude below the pump speed. The turbopump can remove everything that arrives, but almost nothing arrives: S_eff → C_mol, no matter how big the pump is. That is why time-to-base scales so brutally with a small bore at high vacuum — and why d⁴ vs d³ matters far less than the loss of the pressure term.
Mean free path for air at 20 °C: λ = 5×10⁻³ / P cm·Torr — the classic "5 microns at 1 Torr". Regime boundaries drawn at Kn = λ/d of 0.01 (viscous) and 0.5 (molecular).
Model: air at 20 °C; two lumped volumes coupled by C(P̄) = C_viscous + C_molecular (Knudsen-interpolation approximation); turbopump idealized as constant speed S down to a 10⁻⁹ Torr ultimate — a real turbo needs a backing pump and only reaches full speed below ~10⁻² Torr, so read the viscous leg as the roughing phase; laminar viscous flow (no choked-flow correction at the very start). With adhesive outgassing enabled, a 20 cm² H₂O source at 85 °C (q(1 h) = 5×10⁻⁵ Torr·L/s/cm², decaying ∝ 1/t as adsorbed water depletes) feeds the chamber, and H₂O is tracked as its own species — same viscous conductance (bulk flow carries everything), molecular conductance scaled by √(29/18) ≈ 1.27 since C_mol ∝ v̄ ∝ 1/√M. Wall re-adsorption elsewhere is neglected, and the turbo is assumed to pump H₂O at full speed. Integrated with backward Euler on an adaptive log-time grid.
